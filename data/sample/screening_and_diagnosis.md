# Breast cancer screening and diagnosis

Sample educational summary written for the MedTrace demo. Replace with real sources before publishing results.

Mammography is the most common screening test for breast cancer. A mammogram is an X-ray picture of the breast that can find tumors that are too small to feel. Screening mammography can lower the risk of dying from breast cancer in women aged 50 to 69.

Breast MRI uses magnets and radio waves instead of X-rays. MRI is more sensitive than mammography but has more false-positive results, so it is mainly used for women at high risk, such as those with a BRCA1 or BRCA2 mutation. Ultrasound is often used to check a lump found on a mammogram or during a breast exam.

A biopsy is the only way to know for sure whether a breast change is cancer. In a core needle biopsy, a hollow needle removes small pieces of tissue. A pathologist examines the tissue under a microscope and tests it for hormone receptors and HER2.

Staging describes how far the cancer has spread. The TNM system uses tumor size (T), spread to lymph nodes (N), and spread to distant organs (M). A sentinel lymph node biopsy checks whether cancer has reached the first lymph nodes that drain the breast.
