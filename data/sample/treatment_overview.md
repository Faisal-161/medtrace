# Breast cancer treatment overview

Sample educational summary written for the MedTrace demo. Replace with real sources before publishing results.

Treatment depends on the stage of the cancer, its hormone receptor and HER2 status, and the patient's health and preferences. Most people with early breast cancer have surgery. Breast-conserving surgery, also called lumpectomy, removes the tumor and a margin of normal tissue. Mastectomy removes the whole breast.

Radiation therapy uses high-energy rays to kill cancer cells. It is usually given after breast-conserving surgery to lower the chance that the cancer comes back in the breast.

Chemotherapy uses drugs to kill fast-growing cells. Chemotherapy given before surgery is called neoadjuvant therapy and can shrink a tumor. Chemotherapy given after surgery is called adjuvant therapy and lowers the risk of recurrence.

Side effects of chemotherapy can include hair loss, nausea, tiredness, and a higher risk of infection. Many side effects go away after treatment ends.
