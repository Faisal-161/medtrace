# Hormone receptors and HER2 in breast cancer

Sample educational summary written for the MedTrace demo. Replace with real sources before publishing results.

Breast cancers are tested for estrogen receptors (ER), progesterone receptors (PR), and the HER2 protein. These results guide treatment choices. Tumors that have estrogen or progesterone receptors are called hormone receptor-positive. About two out of three breast cancers are hormone receptor-positive.

Hormone receptor-positive breast cancer can be treated with hormone therapy. Tamoxifen blocks estrogen receptors on cancer cells. Aromatase inhibitors such as letrozole and anastrozole lower estrogen levels and are mainly used after menopause.

HER2-positive breast cancer makes too much of the HER2 protein, which helps cancer cells grow. HER2 status is tested with immunohistochemistry (IHC) or in situ hybridization (ISH). HER2-positive breast cancer is treated with HER2-targeted drugs. Trastuzumab is a monoclonal antibody that targets HER2. Pertuzumab is another HER2-targeted antibody that is often given together with trastuzumab.

Triple-negative breast cancer has no estrogen receptors, no progesterone receptors, and does not make too much HER2. Hormone therapy and HER2-targeted drugs do not work against triple-negative breast cancer. Chemotherapy is the main systemic treatment, and immunotherapy may be added for some patients.
