# Breast cancer risk factors and genetics

Sample educational summary written for the MedTrace demo. Replace with real sources before publishing results.

The main risk factors for breast cancer are being female and getting older. Most breast cancers are found in women aged 50 or older. Other risk factors include a family history of breast cancer, dense breast tissue, starting menstrual periods early, starting menopause late, drinking alcohol, and being overweight after menopause.

Inherited mutations in the BRCA1 and BRCA2 genes greatly increase the risk of breast cancer and ovarian cancer. About 5 to 10 percent of breast cancers are linked to inherited gene mutations. People with a strong family history may be offered genetic counseling and genetic testing.

Women with a BRCA1 or BRCA2 mutation may choose more frequent screening, often including breast MRI, or risk-reducing surgery. Mutations in other genes such as PALB2, TP53, and CHEK2 can also raise breast cancer risk.
